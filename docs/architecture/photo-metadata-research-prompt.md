# Photograph metadata research prompt

Retained research prompt, moved from `~/meta-search.md` on 16 November 2026.
Apply it only within the requested research scope and current archive instructions.
It is reference guidance, not a new workflow or permission to adopt metadata.

---

Research the attached historical photographs to enrich their archival metadata. Conduct broad, evidence-based research across independent sources, rather than relying on a few familiar websites.

CONTEXT AND INPUT
The photographs belong to the Industriesalon Schöneweide archive. They may concern WF, TRO, KWO, other industrial enterprises, Berlin history, technical production, training, international delegations and everyday life.

Use the supplied filenames, folder paths, inscriptions, existing descriptions and neighbouring photographs as research clues. Preserve their original wording. Treat existing labels as potentially incomplete or incorrect.

RESEARCH METHOD

1. Begin with visual observations: transcribe readable text and record equipment, architecture, signs, clothing and other identifying details. Separate observations from interpretations.

2. Group related photographs into probable series. Investigate their shared context before researching individual differences. Do not assume that every photograph in a folder depicts the same event or year.

3. Form specific research questions: Which enterprise, site, building, production process, device, event or date might be shown? What evidence could confirm or disprove each hypothesis?

4. Search iteratively using historical and modern names, abbreviations, spelling variants, addresses, technical terminology and relevant languages. Follow useful citations and references beyond the initial search results.

SOURCE BREADTH
Investigate multiple relevant source families:

* Archival finding aids and digitised collections.
* Museum collections and specialist technical collections.
* Historical newspapers, enterprise newspapers, journals and trade publications.
* Books, digitised directories, maps, address books and exhibition catalogues.
* Institutional histories, research publications and dissertations.
* Technical manuals, product catalogues and patents where relevant.
* Local-history projects, specialist associations and eyewitness accounts.

Use collection portals to discover original records, then inspect the underlying institutional source where accessible. Do not restrict research to a predetermined list of websites.

For each series, aim to investigate at least five relevant source families where available. This is a coverage target, not a quota for citations. Several websites repeating the same account count as one evidence chain.

Use enthusiast websites and forums as leads; corroborate their claims where possible. Search snippets alone are not sufficient evidence. State when a promising source could not be accessed.

EVIDENCE RULES

* Clearly distinguish directly visible facts, documented identifications and hypotheses.
* Attach sources to the specific claims they support.
* General historical context does not prove the identity or date of a photograph.
* Seek independent corroboration for precise dates, event identifications and other consequential claims.
* Record contradictions and explain which interpretation is better supported.
* Do not invent precision. Use a justified date range when an exact date is unavailable.
* Do not identify people from facial resemblance alone; require captions or other documentary evidence.
* Keep uncertain claims out of factual titles and descriptions, or explicitly qualify them.

OUTPUT
Write the metadata in German. For each photograph provide:

Dateiname / eindeutige Referenz:
Titel:
Datierung:
Beschreibung:
Ort:
Personen / Organisationen:
Technisches Motiv / Verfahren, falls belegbar:
Schlagwörter: concise terms separated by semicolons
Beleglage: confidence for each major identification, with a brief reason
Quellen: direct links, record identifiers and page references where available
Offene Fragen:

Keep the public description concise and factual. Put research reasoning and uncertainty in the evidence notes.

Also provide a shared research summary for each series, so common context and sources do not need to be repeated in full for every photograph.

COMPLETENESS AND STOPPING
Do not stop after the first plausible match. Check alternative explanations and pursue promising references.

Conclude when the main questions are supported, or when materially different searches and source families produce no further useful evidence. Report:

* Source families and collections investigated.
* Significant unsuccessful searches.
* Access limitations.
* Unresolved questions.
* The most promising next archival enquiry.

An unresolved identification is an acceptable result. A plausible story without evidence is not.
